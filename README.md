# Consensus molecular docking reveals AzoR1 and BsDyP as high-priority candidate enzymes of _Bacillus subtilis_ for potential malachite green transformation
#### Oni David Sunday and Joseph Adewuyi Adeyemi
## Abstract
Consensus molecular docking offers the opportunity to compare the results from different docking engines or methods to reliably assess ligand-target interactions. Here, we assessed the binding activity of malachite green to three _Bacillus subtilis_ enzymes (cotA, AzoR1, and BsDyP) that transform the dye via mechanistically distinct routes. We docked with three independent engines, AutoDock Vina, smina, and Swiss-Dock, under matched search-space parameters. Binding affinities of top poses across the three engines ranged from -7.66 to -5.80 kcal/mol at BsDyP, -7.11 to -6.20 kcal/mol at AzoR1, and 6.77 to 5.70 at cotA.  The engines use different scoring functions; hence, we combined the binding affinities into a rank-based consensus rather than averaging them directly. BsDyP (1.33) and AzoR1 (1.67) consistently outperformed cotA (3.00). Furthermore, the pairwise root mean square deviation between the top pose from each engine was below 0.6 Å at every target, indicating agreement on the same predicted binding mode. We verified pose locations relative to each enzyme's catalytic centre and characterise the intermolecular interactions across all three docking engines. Strikingly, our PLIP analysis identified hydrophobic contacts as the predominant interaction type at every target. Finally, we built a weighted priority framework using binding affinity (40%), cross-engine agreement (25%), catalytic pocket engagement (20%), and interaction quality (15%). This classified AzoR1 and BsDyP as high-priority candidate _B. subtilis_ enzymes (71.0 and 70.0 composite scores, respectively), and cotA as moderate priority (51.0). Our findings support prioritising AzoR1 and BsDyP for experimental validation of malachite green degradation in _B. subtilis_.

##### _Keywords: Bacillus subtilis, malachite green, oxidoreductases, dye-decolourising peroxidase, azoreductase, consensus molecular docking, weighted priority framework_.
