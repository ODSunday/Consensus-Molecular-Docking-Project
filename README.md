# Consensus molecular docking reveals AzoR1 and _Bs_DyP as high-priority candidate enzymes of _Bacillus subtilis_ for plausible malachite green transformation
#### Oni David Sunday and Joseph Adewuyi Adeyemi
## Abstract
Molecular docking offers a time-efficient means of predicting malachite green-enzyme interactions. However, relying on a single docking method can be unreliable. Therefore, comparing results from different docking methods provides a more reliable consensus for predicting the best-performing enzymes in malachite green transformation. Here, we assessed the binding activity of malachite green to three _Bacillus subtilis_ enzymes (cotA, AzoR1, and _Bs_DyP) that transform the dye via mechanistically distinct routes. We docked with three independent engines, AutoDock Vina, smina, and Swiss-Dock, under matched search-space parameters. Binding affinities of top poses across the three engines ranged from -7.66 to -5.80 kcal/mol at _Bs_DyP, -7.11 to -6.20 kcal/mol at AzoR1, and 6.77 to 5.70 at cotA. Because the engines use different scoring functions, we combined the binding affinities into a rank-based consensus rather than averaging them directly. _Bs_DyP (1.33) and AzoR1 (1.67) consistently outperformed cotA (3.00). Furthermore, the pairwise root mean square deviation between the top pose from each engine was below 0.6 Å at every target (0.02 to 0.52 Å), indicating agreement on the same predicted binding mode independent of score agreement alone. We verified pose locations relative to each enzyme's catalytic centre. While AzoR1 poses are localised within a mean of 1.72 Å of the modelled flavin cofactor site, cotA is 6.86 Å from the catalytic copper, compared to its native substrate’s 8.18 Å reference position; _Bs_DyP poses are localised a mean of 11.17 Å from the heme iron despite a docking box centred only 1.92 Å away. Strikingly, our Protein Ligand Interaction Profiler (PLIP) analysis identified hydrophobic contacts as the only interaction type present at any given target: 6 contacts at cotA, 5 at _Bs_DyP and 2 at AzoR1. Finally, we built a weighted priority framework using binding affinity, cross-engine agreement, catalytic pocket engagement, and interaction quality, weighted at 40%, 25%, 20%, and 15%, respectively. This classified AzoR1 and _Bs_DyP as high-priority candidate _B. subtilis_ enzymes, with composite scores of 70.9 and 69.1, respectively, and cotA as moderate priority, with a composite score of 52.3. Therefore, our findings support prioritising AzoR1 and _Bs_DyP for experimental validation of malachite green degradation in _B. subtilis_.

##### _Key words: Bacillus subtilis, malachite green, oxidoreductases, dye-decolourising peroxidase, consensus docking._
